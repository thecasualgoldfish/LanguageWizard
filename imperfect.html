Imperfect Tense (Incomplete past)

The imperfect is a very common tense in Spanish. Thankfully, it is famously one of the easiest to learn! See the chart on the right to see how to conjugate.

What are some common uses of the imperfect tense?

The most common uses of the imperfect tense are

  • Weather
  • Age
  • Time
  • Emotion
  • Repeated actions
  • Setting

You can easily remember this with the acronym 'WATERS'. But what does each one really mean?

Weather

This is pretty self explanatory, you use the imperfect tense when describing the weather in the past.

For example: "Estaba soleado" (It was sunny) << Because you are describing the weather (sunny) in this case, you will use the imperfect tense. Remember "Estar" is used for describing, so you conjugate "estar" in the imperfect, not "ser"

For example: "Hacia calor" (It was hot) << Because you are describing the weather in the past, you use the imperfect. "Hacer calor" means "To be hot", so you conjugate "hacer" in the imperfect.

Age

You also use the imperfect tense when describing age in the past.
For example: "Tenia trece anos" (I was 13 years old) You always use tener when saying how old you are, so you conjugate "tener" in the imperfect. Notice that this also follows the trend of the imperfect being used for description. You use the imperfect when describing things in the past.

Time

You use imperfect when describing the time in the past.
For example: "Eran las tres y media." (It was three thirty) Because you are describing the time in the past, you conjugate "ser" in the imperfect tense (eran). Remember this. Ser is an irregular verb in the imperfect. We will cover this later in the lesson, do not worry!

Emotion

You use the imperfect when describing the emotion of a person in the past.
For example: "Estaba emocionada" (I was excited / She was excited). Because you are describing the emotion of someone (or yourself) in the past, you use the imperfect of estar. You always use estar when talking about emotion because it is a temporary characteristic. If you are wondering why "Estaba emocionada" can be "I" or "She", take a look at your chart and notice that the conjugations for "Yo" and "El/Ella/Usted" are the same!

Repeated Actions

You use the imperfect when talking about something you used to do, habitually/repeatedly in the past. The imperfect tense makes a verb conjugate most closely to "used to" or "was/were", so the imperfect makes the most sense to use.
For example: "Ibamos a la playa todos los dias" (We used to go to the beach every day) This is describing a past habitual action, so we will use the imperfect!

Setting

You use the imperfect when describing the features of a place and "setting the scene".
For example: "La calle estaba tranquila" (The street was calm) Because you are describing the setting of a place, (How the street was calm), you will use the imperfect.
Now, there are a few irregular verbs in the imperfect, but thankfully, only three! These should be easy to remember.

Ser

Yo: Era
Tú: Eras
Él/Ella/Usted: Era
Ellos/Ustedes: Eran
Nosotros: Eramos

Ir

Yo: Iba
Tú: Ibas
Él/Ella/Usted: Iba
Ellos/Ustedes: Ibas
Nosotros: Ibamos

Ver

Yo: Veia
Tú: Veias
Él/Ella/Usted: Veia
Ellos/Ustedes: Veian
Nosotros: Veiamos

There are NO other irregularities in the imperfect tense. Even in stem changing verbs like "tener", you conjugate from the infinitive. For example: "tener" >> "tenia".
