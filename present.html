Present Tense

The present tense is the most common tense in spanish, thankfully, it can be pretty simple! See the chart on the right to see how to conjugate.

What are some common uses of the present tense?

The most common uses of the preterite tense are

  • Habits and routines
  • Universal Truths
  • Going on now
  • Scheduled / Near future

You can easily remember this with the acronym 'HUGS'. But what does each one really mean?

Habits and Routines

Actions you do regularly / habitually. If it helps, the uses are pretty much the same in Spanish as in English. When talking about your routines, you use the present tense.

For example: "Corro todos los dias" (I run everyday) Because running is something you do everyday / regularly you use the present tense. It doesn't have to happen everyday, you could still use the present tense when talking about something you do every Tuesday, for instance.

Universal Truths

You use the present tense when talking about something that is always true, or known to be true.
For example: "El sol sale por el este" (The sun rises in the east) << Because you are stating it as a fact, you use the present tense.

Going on now

You use the present tense when talking about something you are doing right in the moment.
For example: "Hablo contigo" (I am talking with you) Becuase you are currently speaking with the person, you use the present tense.

Scheduled / Near Future

You use the present tense when telling about something that will occur in the near future / something that has been scheduled.
For example: "Manana viajo a Madrid" (Tomorrow I travel to Madrid) Because you are doing this action in the near future, while stating what time you are planning to do it, you use the present tense.
Don't worry if it seems confusing at first, it does for almost everyone! Soon, you will be a master of the present tense. Remember that these uses are very similar to English!

Irregular Verbs

Knowing the conjugations is most of the present tense, but there are still some irregular verbs you should know. These are the most common.

Ir (To go)

Yo: Voy
Tú: Vas
Él/Ella/Usted: Va
Nosotros: Vamos
Ellos/Ustedes: Van

To be

Spanish has 2 verbs for "to be". This can be confusing at first, but they each have their own use cases. Once you learn these, it will be a walk in the park!

Ser (To be)

Ser is used to describe permanent characteristics. Nationality, permanent character/physical traits, relationship, occupation, date and time, possession, event location, what something is made of.
Yo: Soy
Tú: Eres
Él/Ella/Usted: Es
Ellos/Ustedes: Son
Nosotros: Somos

Estar (To be)

Estar is used when talking about temporary characteristics. Emotions, where someone/something is, physical condition / state, position and posture, and ongoing actions.
Yo: Estoy
Tú: Estas
Él/Ella/Usted: Esta
Ellos/Ustedes: Estan
Nosotros: Estamos

Stem Changing Verbs

Some verbs in the present tense have something called a stem change. This means that when you are conjugating the verb from the infinitive, you must also add the stem change.

How it works

There is no way to tell if verbs are stem changing, so unfortunately you will have to memorize then and how to conjugate them. Thankfully, most verbs do not have this, and it is not extremely hard!
Some verbs have a u -> ue stem change.
For example: Jugar (to play) when conjugated in the yo form goes to "Juego" (I play). The "u" from the infinitive changed to a ue when conjugated. HOWEVER this stem change does not occur in Nosotros and "We play" is "Jugamos".

These are all the verbs with a u -> ue stem change. They are sorted from most to least common. Try and memorize the first 5!

  • Jugar (To play)

Now that you've noted those, there is another common type of stem change. The e -> ie. This stem change works the same as u -> ue, so it should be easy!

For example: the verb "sentir" (to feel) has an e -> ie stem change. Do you know how to say I feel? Thats right! "Yo siento." The stem change does not apply to Nosotros here either.
There can also be changes at the end of the verb. For instance, the verb "Preferir" (to prefer) has a e _ -> ie change on the second e. Thus, "I prefer" would be "Yo prefiero".

Reflexive Verbs

You may have already seen some verbs that end in "se". These are reflexive verbs.

What are reflexive verbs?

Reflexive verbs mean the action is being done to someone or something. For example "sentirse". "Sentir" means to feel, but when you use the reflexive verb version, you are stating that someone or something feels When conjugating reflexive verbs, you will use corresponding reflexive pronouns.

What are the reflexive pronouns?

  • Yo: Me
  • Tú: Te
  • Él/Ella/Usted: Le
  • Ellos/Ustedes: Les
  • Nosotros: Nos

How do you conjugate reflexive verbs?

It is quite simple, actually! Take off the "se" and conjugate as you would usually. Then, replace the pronoun with the reflexive pronoun. The reflexive verb can also go before the verb. For instance, "sentirse". -> "sentir" -> "siento" in the "yo" form. Then add the reflexive verb. It would be "me siento".

When to use these pronouns?

You can put the reflexive verb at the beginning, like "me siento" or they can be attached to the end. There are only a few cases when this is true.
